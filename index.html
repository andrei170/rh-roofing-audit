Twelve Pages, Four Thousand Words, Six Projects — And Not One Phone Number A Customer Can Tap.

0
tappable phone links across all twelve pages

Most roofing enquiries are a call, made from a phone, by somebody standing in a garden looking up.

Your number is printed as text on every page. It is not a link, so tapping it does nothing. They have to memorise it, leave your site, open the keypad and type it.

A good proportion will not. This is a five-minute change and it is the highest-return item here.

Your published sitemap lists one page. That is why a quick look makes this site seem far smaller than it is — and it is worth fixing, because search engines use that file too.

You have six project pages, including a school and a villa in France. A 671-word feedback page. A services page. An enquiry form on the contact page. That is real material, and most roofers have none of it.

Finding 1 — The Phone Number Is Not A Link. Anywhere.
Pages checked 12 Pages with a tappable number 0 Published on the site: 01392 841167 landline 07703 989767 mobile
This is the one to do today

On a phone, a number written as plain text does nothing when touched. Every other roofer they are comparing you against will have a number that dials when tapped.

Roofing is an urgent trade. A leak, a slipped slate, a storm — those calls are made in the moment, outdoors, one-handed. Making both numbers proper links is minutes of work for whoever manages the site.

Second question while you are there: which number is the business? Two numbers split your listings and your reviews over time, and neither is tracked.

Finding 2 — Nothing Is Measured At All
Google Analytics not found Google Tag Manager not found Google Ads tag not found Meta pixel not found Call tracking not found Structured data not found
You have twelve pages and no idea which of them anybody reads

There is no tracking of any kind. Nobody knows how many people visit, whether they reach the feedback page, or whether the form is ever used.

That matters because you have material worth measuring. If the King's School page is what wins commercial enquiries, you would want more like it. Right now that is unknowable. Analytics is free and takes minutes.

Finding 3 — The Sitemap Says One Page
Pages that actually exist 12 Pages in your published sitemap 1
A small technical thing with a real effect

A sitemap is the file that tells search engines what exists on a site. Yours lists the homepage and nothing else. Your project pages, your feedback page and your services page are not in it.

Search engines can still find pages by following links, so this is not fatal — but you are making them work for it, and there is no reason to. It is a one-off fix.

Finding 4 — The One From August, And It Has Not Changed
We looked at this market for you on 25 August. This is still the biggest gap.

TrustATrader lists ten roofers covering Exeter. Checkatrade lists forty-eight. You are on neither.

That analysis is still live and worth rereading before we speak — it named every listed firm, their ratings and their review counts. The bottom of that list included firms with five and nine reviews, ranking above you, because they have a profile and you do not.

It also showed what the market leader does: one membership, listed under seven separate categories — roofing, lead, roof coating, chimneys, rubber roofing, guttering, roof repair. You do every one of those.

What We Would Do, In Order
  • Make both numbers tappable, on all twelve pages. Minutes of work, and the difference between a call and a closed tab.
  • Put analytics on, so within a month you know which of your twelve pages anybody actually reads.
  • Get listed on Checkatrade and TrustATrader, under every category you serve, not just roofing. That is the August finding and it is still the biggest single gap.
  • Fix the sitemap so all twelve pages are declared.
  • Write a page for Exeter and the Devon towns you want to work in.
  • Turn the six projects into a proper case-study set — the King's School one is a commercial credential most domestic roofers cannot match, and it is buried.

A school roof, a villa in France, twelve pages of work — and no way to tap your number.

You are not short of substance. You are short of the small things that let people act on it.
